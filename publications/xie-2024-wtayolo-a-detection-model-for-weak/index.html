WTAYOLO: A Detection Model for Weak Scratches on Metal Surfaces

Weijin Xie, Wei Pan, Simi Li, Qi Zeng, Huayong Li
Proceedings of the 2024 7th International Conference on Sensors, Signal and Image Processing, 22-28
{ } BibTeX DOI

In one sentenceWTAYOLO finds faint scratches on shiny lithium-battery surfaces: photometric stereo imaging reduces interference from surface reflections, and a WTAConv module strengthens scratch features, reaching 86.8% mAP50 without extra parameters.

The problem

Scratches on the surface of lithium batteries significantly affect their electrochemical performance and operational safety, so stringent scratch detection is crucial for product quality. Detection remains difficult, however: lithium battery materials are reflective, and the morphology of the scratches themselves makes them hard for detection algorithms to pick up.

How it works

WTAYOLO is a detection model for weak scratches on metal surfaces. It combines photometric stereo imaging, which tackles the reflections, with a new module, WTAConv, which improves how scratch features are extracted from the images.

  1. Photometric stereo imaging. WTAYOLO uses a photometric stereo system, which reduces interference from surface reflections.
  2. A wider receptive field. The WTAConv module expands the receptive field, strengthening the extraction of scratch features from the images.
  3. Channel and spatial attention. Within WTAConv, channel and spatial attention mechanisms strengthen important features and suppress redundant information.
  4. Scratch detection. The model detects the subtle scratches.

Results

mAP50 of 86.8 %, a 5.1 % improvement over the YOLOv11n baseline, without increasing the number of parameters.

The authors conclude that the method strikes an effective balance between accuracy and model cost-effectiveness, providing an effective solution for industrial applications.

This page summarises the paper’s abstract. The full paper is available from the publisher: doi.org/10.1145/3725949.3725956.
