Learning-based reconstruction of moving transparent objects

Hua Feng, Lei Lu, Zhilong Su, Wei Pan
International Conference on Optoelectronic Science and Intelligent Sensing (SPIE), 1417511
{ } BibTeX DOI

In one sentenceFringes projected onto a transparent object reflect off both its front and rear surfaces and overlap. A diffusion model separates them; SIFT and 2D motion equations then reconstruct the moving object.

The problem

Transparent moving objects have long been challenging targets in optical imaging: they have high transmittance and weak texture, and their imaging involves both refraction and reflection. Unlike conventional opaque rigid objects, a transparent object reflects the fringe patterns projected by phase-shifting profilometry (PSP) from both its front and rear surfaces, so the captured images show severe fringe superposition. When the object moves, its refraction effects are further coupled with motion-induced errors, which makes accurate reconstruction significantly harder.

How it works

The paper systematically analyzes how the imaging mechanisms of transparent objects influence geometric modeling and motion estimation, and proposes a learning-based reconstruction method for moving transparent objects that works in two stages.

  1. Superimposed fringes. PSP fringe patterns are reflected by both the front and rear surfaces of the moving transparent object and overlap in the captured images.
  2. Fringe separation. A diffusion model separates the superimposed fringe patterns.
  3. Reconstruction of the moving object. The moving object is then reconstructed based on the SIFT algorithm and two-dimensional motion equations.

Results

Experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the method in complex dynamic transparent scenarios.
